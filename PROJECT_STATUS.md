### Why 100 does not mean perfect execution
- The scoring function has a flat 100-point band below each good threshold, then a linear decline. Current SCALE=1.5 makes the effective all-100 tolerances:
  - pelvis displacement from shoulder–ankle line: <=4.5% of body length;
  - whole-body lean: <=4.5 degrees;
  - shoulder deficit: <=12 degrees;
  - elbow deficit: <=12 degrees;
  - robust sway: <=1.5% of body length.
- Hence nonzero deviations, e.g. pelvis 3%, lean 2 degrees, shoulder/elbow deficits 8 degrees and sway 1%, all map to 100 on their respective metrics. The policy deliberately tolerates small deviations/noise, but cannot distinguish different very-good performances inside those flat bands. It does not establish anatomical or technical perfection.
- Hold weights remain pelvis .30, lean .20, shoulder .20, elbow .15, sway .15; available phases remain renormalized. Integer rounding adds another ambiguity: a hold total 99.6 displays 100; 96.8 displays 97 (confirmed using the production overallHandstandScore helper). Those are illustrative inputs, NOT recovered raw totals from the real clips.
- The full wall clip and seconds 6–11 excerpt cover different intervals; hold selection/medians, sampling and the excerpt's re-encoding can change derived values. These are plausible sources of the 97 versus 100 difference, not measured attribution. Neither an arbitrary score reduction nor a confidence-threshold relaxation is justified by the retained evidence.
